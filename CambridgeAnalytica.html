Introduction

Would you hand over your profile?

What if five dollars allowed someone to see your Facebook information, build a psychological profile, and test whether targeted content could change your political views?

The survey looked simple. The consequences reached the people who participated, their friends, and eventually the democratic process.

Source: Amer & Noujaim, 2019

Readability

Some controls are clear. The full story is not.

Clear enough to use

  • Privacy Checkup provides guided toggles.
  • Users can adjust who sees their posts.
  • Security and ad preferences are grouped together.
  • Some pages now include AI-generated summaries.

Still muddy

  • Long answers and jargon overwhelm users.
  • The old “Protecting Your Information” label is difficult to find.
  • Exporting data does not explain every inference.
  • Finding a control is not the same as understanding the consequences.

Incident Continuance Question

Why will this keep happening?

Because personal data creates money, targeting, and power.

Social media companies benefit from knowing what users may buy, believe, or vote for. As long as that information remains valuable, organizations will continue trying to collect more of it.

Source: Amer & Noujaim, 2019
Conclusion

Deleting a profile does not erase the question

The scandal happened during a seven-year break from social media. Returning made it clear how much the platforms had changed—and how difficult it was to remove years of posts and pictures.

There is no complete confidence that deleting a profile means every copy, prediction, or digital avatar is gone. The scandal was not only about a quiz. It was about control over the information that defines people.
