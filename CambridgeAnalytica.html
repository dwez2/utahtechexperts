Introduction

What would you give away for five dollars?

Cambridge Analytica used a personality quiz to collect Facebook data. People volunteered to take the survey, but the data collection reached beyond the people who completed it.

The real privacy question was not only what users knowingly shared. It was what the platform allowed others to collect, combine, analyze, and use to influence behavior.

Source: Amer & Noujaim, 2019
Incident Summary

A quiz opened the door to millions of profiles

Users connected a personality survey to Facebook. The application collected information from participants and, through Facebook’s platform design at the time, information connected to many of their friends.

$5 surveyPeople were offered a small payment to complete a personality survey.
87 millionFacebook later said as many as 87 million users may have had data shared.
Political targetingPsychographic profiles supported highly targeted political messaging.
Sources: Amer & Noujaim, 2019; NBC News, 2018
Incident Summary · Continued

How did the data become influence?

Quiz

A user completed a personality survey and connected it to Facebook.

Collect

The application collected participant information and accessible friend data.

Combine

Data points were combined to create detailed psychological profiles.

Target

Campaigns could divide audiences into groups and deliver different messages.

Influence

Content was designed to move behavior, opinions, engagement, or voting choices.

Sources: Amer & Noujaim, 2019; PBS, n.d.
Legal Ramifications

The scandal produced historic penalties

United KingdomThe Information Commissioner’s Office imposed the maximum penalty available under the law at the time.
United StatesIn 2019, the Federal Trade Commission approved a $5 billion settlement with Facebook.
Regulatory oversightThe case raised questions about consent, platform responsibility, third-party access, and whether privacy promises were followed.
Continuing litigationYears later, Cambridge Analytica-related claims and questions about Facebook’s disclosures continued through the courts.
Sources: FTC, 2019; Vijayan, 2024
Ethical Ramifications

Legal access did not make the use ethical

Informed consentMany affected users did not knowingly agree to have their information used for political profiling.
Third-party responsibilityFacebook did not adequately control how an outside organization obtained and used platform data.
ManipulationHighly personalized political messaging could exploit fear, personality, or uncertainty instead of supporting informed decisions.
AccountabilityFacebook, developers, campaigns, and Cambridge Analytica each pointed toward other parties when responsibility was questioned.
Sources: Amer & Noujaim, 2019; NBC News, 2018
Privacy Policies and Notices

“Protecting Your Information” came after the exposure

87 million noticesFacebook notified users who might have had their data shared with Cambridge Analytica.
Platform-wide noticeAll Facebook users also received information about reviewing applications connected to their accounts.
Privacy CheckupFacebook now provides guided controls for sharing, account security, data settings, and advertising preferences.
Timing mattersA notice issued after data has already been collected cannot provide meaningful consent before the harm occurs.
Transparency mattersUsers need to understand what was collected, who received it, why it was used, and how to remove access.
Sources: NBC News, 2018; CBS News, 2018
Readability and User Control

Privacy controls exist, but finding answers is still difficult

Too much informationLong privacy explanations and technical language can overwhelm users who are trying to make a simple choice.
Ad preferencesUsers can change categories and settings, but they may not see a clear list explaining exactly how their profile is targeted.
Third-party applicationsUsers need a simple way to identify connected applications and remove access.
Deleting dataRemoving posts or deleting a profile does not necessarily prove that every copy, profile, or inference has disappeared.
Effective choiceA privacy control only helps when an ordinary person can find it, understand it, and use it.
Sources: Facebook privacy controls reviewed for the original paper; CBS News, 2018
Significant Privacy Issues

Privacy settings do not always explain the full picture

  • Facebook provides privacy settings, Privacy Checkup, and advertising preferences.
  • Users can review connected applications and remove access.
  • Privacy pages explain what information the platform collects.
  • Videos describe personalization as a way to make content more useful.
  • Users can change some categories used for advertising.
  • The controls can create the feeling that the user has complete control.
  • Users may not see a clear list of every advertisement category assigned to them.
  • Ordinary activity can be combined to create predictions the user never provided directly.
  • Data can be copied and used outside the platform where the original controls no longer apply.
  • Deleting an account does not prove that every copied record or inference disappeared.
  • Political targeting can show different messages to different groups without public visibility.
  • Privacy choices become less meaningful when the important details are difficult to find.
Sources: Facebook privacy controls reviewed for the original paper; Amer & Noujaim, 2019
Other Incidents

The controversy did not end with Cambridge Analytica

2018–2019

Promises and penalties

Facebook announced privacy changes, while regulators imposed penalties and additional oversight.

2024

Litigation continued

Facebook asked the Supreme Court to dismiss a Cambridge Analytica-related shareholder lawsuit.

October 2026

New Mexico cases

Meta faced major judgments involving user data privacy and claims about the safety of social media for children.

Sources: CBS News, 2018; Vijayan, 2024; Hays, 2026
Incident Continuance Question

Why could this happen again?

01

Data

Platforms continue collecting activity, interests, relationships, and behavior.

02

Profiles

Separate details are combined into predictions about personality and likely actions.

03

Targeting

Advertisers and campaigns pay to reach carefully selected groups.

04

Influence

Messages can be designed to affect what people buy, believe, or vote for.

If data can predict behavior, how much power should companies have to use it to influence what people buy, believe, or vote for?
Sources: Amer & Noujaim, 2019; Cadwalladr, 2019
Conclusion

The scandal was about more than one quiz

This case changed how social media looks after returning from a seven-year break.

Deleting years of posts showed how easy it is to create a digital history and how difficult it is to take it back. There is still no complete confidence that deleting a profile means every copy and every derived profile is gone.

Read the original midterm paper ↗

Cambridge Analytica showed how information collected through an ordinary platform could be combined, transformed into psychological profiles, and used for a purpose that many people never expected.

“Privacy is power” and “data rights are human rights.”

Sources: Cadwalladr, 2019; Amer & Noujaim, 2019
